\begin{abstract}
The mobility selector \code{BCC_nl_Eshelby_Resist} preserves the original BCC
nonlinear constitutive law outside Eshelby inclusions.  When the dislocation
node itself lies inside any periodic ellipsoidal inclusion, the dimensionless
parameter \code{MobEshelbyResist} multiplies the complete BCC nonlinear
resistance, including both force and Jacobian terms.  This document derives
the binary point test, resistance-scaled force balance, Newton matrix, limiting
cases, source integration, and required control-file input.  The implementation
is a complete standalone source file; neither \path{MobilityLaw_BCC_nl.cc} nor
the existing arm-fraction \code{BCC_nl_Eshelby} law is changed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Binary node membership}

\subsection{Periodic ellipsoid equation}

Let the node position be \(\vect{x}\).  Inclusion \(p\) has center
\(\vect{C}_p\), positive semi-axes \(a_{p0},a_{p1},a_{p2}\), and principal
directions \(\vect{e}_{p0},\vect{e}_{p1}\).  The third direction is
\begin{equation}
 \vect{e}_{p2}=
 \frac{\vect{e}_{p0}\times\vect{e}_{p1}}
      {\norm{\vect{e}_{p0}\times\vect{e}_{p1}}}.
\end{equation}
The source first applies the ParaDiS minimum-image operation,
\begin{equation}
 \vect{d}_p=\operatorname{ZImage}(\vect{x}-\vect{C}_p),
 \label{eq:zimage}
\end{equation}
then evaluates the squared unit-sphere coordinate
\begin{equation}
 \chi_p(\vect{x})=
 \sum_{k=0}^{2}
 \left(\frac{\vect{e}_{pk}\mathbin{\cdot}\vect{d}_p}{a_{pk}}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
This is the same ellipsoid-to-sphere transform used by
\path{src/SegPartIntersect.cc}.

The binary indicator is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 I(\vect{x})=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&\min_p\chi_p(\vect{x})\leq1+10^{-12},\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}}
 \label{eq:indicator}
\end{equation}
The tolerance classifies a node on the particle boundary as inside.  Locally
owned and ghost inclusions are both scanned through
\begin{center}
\code{home->totInclusionCount}.
\end{center}
Overlapping particles do not compound the resistance: membership in any one
particle sets \(I=1\).

\subsection{Why this is a node test}

This law deliberately does not use segment--particle intersection lengths.
A node inside a particle is scaled even if its connected arms immediately
leave the particle.  Conversely, a node outside remains on ordinary BCC
nonlinear mobility even when one of its arms crosses a particle.

This differs from \code{BCC_nl_Eshelby}, which computes an endpoint-weighted
covered fraction for every arm and uses a BCC\_0 tensor on covered portions.
The two selectors implement different physical models and coexist.

\section{Outside constitutive law: BCC nonlinear mobility}

\subsection{Thermally activated screw relation}

The standalone file contains its own copy of the original BCC nonlinear
helpers and initializer.  Define
\[
 p=\code{MobExpP},\qquad q=\code{MobExpQ},\qquad
 \alpha_0=\code{MobAlpha},\qquad
 H=\frac{\code{MobDeltaH0}}{k_BT},
\]
and \(P_{\rm GPa}=P/10^9\).  Initialization computes
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L
 &=A C_0\left(1-C\frac{T}{T_m}+D P_{\rm GPa}\right),
 \label{eq:L}\\
 y&=\alpha_0^p,\nonumber\\
 \mathcal N
 &=\frac{\mathcal L}
 {\exp[-H(1-y)^q]\,[1+pqH(1-y)^{q-1}y]},
 \label{eq:N}\\
 V_0
 &=\alpha_0\mathcal L
   -\alpha_0\mathcal N\exp[-H(1-y)^q].
 \label{eq:V0}
\end{align}
Here \(A,C_0,C,D,T_m\) correspond to \code{MobCoeffA},
\code{MobCoeffC0}, \code{MobCoeffC}, \code{MobCoeffD}, and
\code{meltTemp}.

For a screw Burgers family, let
\begin{equation}
 M_L=\frac{\mathcal L}{b_{\rm mag}},\qquad
 M_N=\frac{\mathcal N}{b_{\rm mag}},\qquad
 v_0=\frac{V_0}{b_{\rm mag}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 f_P=\code{MobPeierls}\,\norm{\vect b},\qquad
 \omega=\frac{M_N}{f_P},\qquad
 v_{\rm tr}=\alpha_0M_L-v_0.
\end{equation}
Below the transition, the scalar screw glide force is the numerical inverse of
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 v=\omega f\,
 \exp\left[-H\left(1-(f/f_P)^p\right)^q\right],
 \qquad 0\leq v<v_{\rm tr}.}
 \label{eq:thermal}
\end{equation}
Above the transition the continuation is
\begin{equation}
 f(v)=\frac{(v+v_0)f_P}{M_L}.
 \label{eq:linear-continuation}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Nodal BCC nonlinear resistance}

For arm \(i\), let \(L_i\) be its length, \(\vect{t}_i\) its unit tangent,
and \(\vect b_i\) its Burgers vector.  Its projected lengths are
\begin{equation}
 L_{s,i}=\frac{\abs{\vect r_i\mathbin{\cdot}\vect b_i}}
                     {\norm{\vect b_i}},
 \qquad
 L_{e,i}=\sqrt{\max(0,L_i^2-L_{s,i}^2)}.
\end{equation}

The source groups collinear screw Burgers vectors.  Its complete unscaled
nodal resistance may be written
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R_{\rm nl}(\vect v)
 =D_{\rm lin}\vect v+
  \sum_g C_g\,\vect F_{s,g}(\vect v),}
 \label{eq:base-resistance}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 C_g=\frac12\sum_{i\in g}L_{s,i}.
\end{equation}
\(D_{\rm lin}\) contains the \(L_e/2\) edge tensors and \(L/2\) junction
tensors.  The nonlinear screw force \(\vect F_{s,g}\) contains the scalar law
in Equations~\eqref{eq:thermal}--\eqref{eq:linear-continuation} in the plane
normal to \(\vect b_g\), plus line drag along \(\vect b_g\).

\section{Inside resistance multiplier}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\code{MobEshelbyResist}>0
\end{equation}
and define the nodal scale
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\beta(\vect{x})=1+(\alpha-1)I(\vect{x}).}
 \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\beta=1\) outside and \(\beta=\alpha\) inside.  The new nodal force
balance is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \vect r(\vect v)=
 \vect F_{\rm node}-\beta\mathcal R_{\rm nl}(\vect v)=\vect0.}
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Its Newton Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 J(\vect v)=
 -\beta\left[
 D_{\rm lin}+
 \sum_g C_g\frac{\partial\vect F_{s,g}}{\partial\vect v}
 \right].}
 \label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}

The source assembles ordinary BCC\_nl first.  Immediately before the
one-screw shortcut and Newton loop, it multiplies every component of
\code{dflindv} and every populated \code{dfndfscrew[g]} by \(\beta\).  This
scales linear edge/junction drag, nonlinear screw force, derivative, line
solve, and returned inverse Jacobian exactly once.

Equation~\eqref{eq:residual} also gives the useful identity
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \vect r_\alpha(\vect v;\vect F)
 =\alpha\,\vect r_1(\vect v;\vect F/\alpha)
 \quad\text{for an inside node}.}
 \label{eq:force-equivalence}
\end{equation}
Therefore the inside solution is ordinary BCC\_nl evaluated at an effective
force \(\vect F/\alpha\); no new screw interpolation is introduced.

\section{Interpretation and limits}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}P{0.28\textwidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
Condition & Result \\
\midrule
\(I=0\) & Exact original BCC\_nl execution and algebra. \\
\(I=1,\ \alpha=1\) & Exact original BCC\_nl despite being inside. \\
\(I=1,\ \alpha>1\) & Increased resistance and reduced mobility. \\
\(I=1,\ 0<\alpha<1\) & Reduced resistance and increased mobility. \\
\(\alpha\leq0\), NaN, or infinity & Rejected during mobility initialization. \\
Empty \code{inclusionFile} & \(I=0\) for every node; ordinary BCC\_nl. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\code{MobEshelbyResist} is dimensionless.  It is not a velocity and not an
absolute mobility.  It follows the established ParaDiS Eshelby convention
\[
 B_{\rm inside}=\alpha B_{\rm outside}.
\]

When \code{useLabFrame} is enabled, Equation~\eqref{eq:indicator} is still
evaluated in simulation coordinates before any crystal-frame mobility
rotation.  The force, velocity, arm, and Burgers vectors then follow the
ordinary BCC\_nl rotation path.

\section{Control-file input}

The following block can replace the material/mobility section of a BCC
nonlinear control file.  The numerical values are the existing tantalum-like
defaults and must be replaced by the calibration for the intended material.

\begin{verbatim}
# Enable particle geometry and select the binary nodal law
mobilityLaw = "BCC_nl_Eshelby_Resist"
inclusionFile = "tests/screwEsh.dat"
materialTypeName = "BCC"

# Dimensionless inside drag multiplier
# 1.0: no change; values greater than 1 reduce inside mobility
MobEshelbyResist = 1.0e5

# Thermodynamic state
TempK = 600.0
pressure = 0.0

# BCC_nl linear mobilities outside and inside
# Inside values are converted to drag and multiplied by MobEshelbyResist
MobGlide = 1.0e2
MobLine = 1.0e4
MobClimb = 1.0e-5

# BCC_nl nonlinear screw parameters
MobPeierls = 3.240000e8
MobDeltaH0 = 1.728748e-19
MobAlpha = 9.661236e-1
MobExpP = 0.5
MobExpQ = 1.23
MobCoeffA = 1.525
MobCoeffC = 2.032370e-1
MobCoeffC0 = 2.048000e3
MobCoeffD = 1.626950e-4
meltTemp = 3.170683e3

# Optional nonlinear-property files; empty uses the values above
MobFileNL = ""
ecFile = ""
meltTempFile = ""
\end{verbatim}

For the repository's screw/inclusion data, a one-step serial smoke test is
\begin{verbatim}
bin/paradis -maxstep 1 -s -d tests/screwEsh.data \
  path/to/BCC_nl_Eshelby_Resist.ctrl
\end{verbatim}

Unlike the BCC\_0-inside hybrid, this law does not read \code{MobScrew} or
\code{MobEdge}.  Its only new constitutive input is
\code{MobEshelbyResist}; all other mobility inputs are inherited from
BCC\_nl.

\section{Registration and restart behavior}

The selector is registered under \code{ESHELBY} with BCC, non-planar,
no-fuzzy-plane, and no-cross-slip attributes matching \code{BCC_nl}.
\path{src/DisableUnneededParams.cc} retains:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \code{inclusionFile} and \code{MobEshelbyResist};
 \item \code{pressure}, \code{TempK}, and all BCC nonlinear constants;
 \item \code{MobGlide}, \code{MobLine}, and \code{MobClimb};
 \item optional \code{MobFileNL}, \code{ecFile}, and \code{meltTempFile}.
\end{itemize}
No new parameter binding is needed.  \code{MobEshelbyResist} and
\code{inclusionFile} are already defined by the existing parameter layer.

\section{Verification and cautions}

The implementation was checked with:
\begin{itemize}
 \item syntax compilation with and without \code{ESHELBY};
 \item complete serial links in both build modes;
 \item symbol inspection showing the selector and public functions only in the
       ESHELBY binary;
 \item a one-step ParaDiS run using \path{tests/screwEsh.data} and
       \path{tests/screwEsh.dat};
 \item source-level checks of \(\beta=1\), outside, and
       Equation~\eqref{eq:force-equivalence};
 \item periodic, rotated-ellipsoid, boundary, and overlap analysis of the
       point-membership equation.
\end{itemize}

The membership loop scans the locally available inclusion array for every
mobile node.  This guarantees that node-only and zero-length-arm cases do not
depend on segment intersection lists, but its cost grows with the number of
local and ghost inclusions.  If very large particle populations make this
dominant, a spatial point-query index can replace the scan without changing
Equations~\eqref{eq:indicator}--\eqref{eq:jacobian}.

The copied BCC\_nl solver intentionally preserves the original law's numerical
branches so the outside limit remains exact.  Baseline nonlinear-solver
changes should be applied separately and consistently to both source files.
